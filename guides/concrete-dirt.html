Concrete and dirt: weight, not yards

Concrete, brick, dirt, gravel, and stone are not household junk. They are heavy. The usual box is a 10-yard (sometimes a 12- or 15-yard) with a low tonnage cap. A 30- or 40-yard of dirt is how people crack a truck scale and a driveway.

Weight warning. A cubic yard of concrete or wet dirt can weigh well over a ton. Haulers often refuse large boxes for clean fill, or they will not pull an overloaded one until you shovel it off. Book a smaller dumpster on purpose.

Starting size

  • Patio, walk, small slab, dirt from a trench: 10-yard heavy-debris box.
  • More volume only if the company says so: 12- or 15-yard, still with a tight cap.
  • Do not book a 30 or 40 for concrete or dirt unless the hauler explicitly allows it for that material.

Keep concrete “clean” if you want a clean-fill rate — no household trash, no wood, no plastic. Mixed loads get the expensive ticket. Brick and block are the same conversation as concrete. Dirt with roots and sod may be treated as mixed waste.

The truck is heavier than the dumpster. Boards under the rails help a driveway; they do not help a lawn. Street placement is a city permit question; driveway vs street.

If the job is a kitchen or house cleanout with a little leftover tile, that is still a remodel dumpster — see kitchen — not a dirt box. Size guide. Overage is the real bill.
