Project guide

Dumpster for a roof tear-off

A single-house asphalt roof usually wants a 20-yard dumpster. The limiter is weight, not how tall the pile looks from the driveway.

Asphalt shingles are dense. One square (100 sq ft) of tear-off is often 250–400 pounds depending on layers and whether you are pulling felt and decking. A 2,000 sq ft roof with one old layer can land in the 3–5 ton range before you add drip edge and trash. That is why haulers cap tons in the quote, and why a 30- or 40-yard is the wrong default for shingles.

Starting size

  • Most asphalt tear-offs: 20-yard. Book it when you book the crew so the box is empty on day one.
  • Small ranch, one layer, modest footprint: a 15-yard can work if the hauler allows shingles in that size.
  • Two stories, steep pitch, two old layers, tile or slate: ask before you assume a 20. Tile and slate are much heavier; many haulers want a 10-yard and a lower cap, or a dedicated heavy-debris box.

Weight beats volume. If the quote’s included tons are low, a cheap 20-yard is an expensive overage. Line up size, tons, and days — not the drop fee alone.

How to load it

Keep household junk out of a roofing dumpster unless the hauler says mixed debris is fine. Nails belong in the box, not in the lawn; a magnetic sweep is on the roofing crew, not on us. Do not overfill above the fill line — the truck cannot legally tarp a peaked load.

Place the box on a driveway when you can. Street drops need a city conversation; driveway vs street. Protect the drive if the hauler agrees — the truck is heavier than the dumpster.
