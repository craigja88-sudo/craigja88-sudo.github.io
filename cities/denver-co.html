Denver, Colorado

Dumpster rental in Denver

Denver dumpsters deal with snow, freeze-thaw, and a Front Range construction season that packs into the months when the ground is workable.

Winter storms delay drops and pickups; ice on a driveway is a safety issue for the truck, not just for you. Altitude does not change cubic yards. Semi-arid weather does mean uncovered loads can be dusty — use tarps the way the hauler instructs. The City and County of Denver, inner suburbs (Lakewood, Aurora, Westminster), and mountain-adjacent towns are different jurisdictions. Mountain jobs can add mileage.

Alley-loaded U-Haul neighborhoods and narrow Victorian lots downtown are access-limited. A 30- or 40-yard may not fit even if the debris volume wants one. We will not invent Denver’s right-of-way fee.

Sizes that actually get used

Same US sizes as everywhere: 10, 15, 20, 30, and 40 cubic yards. Most Denver home projects land on a 10-yard (bathroom, small deck, heavy debris) or a 20-yard (roof, cleanout, kitchen-plus). Size guide and chooser.

Pricing recap

Front Range pricing often runs at or a bit above the middle of national ranges, and distance into the foothills is a real adder. Extra winter days happen; ask how weather delays are billed. National context: many 10-yard weeks quote around $250–$500 and 20-yard weeks around $350–$700 before overage, extra days, and trip fees. Not a Denver guarantee. What changes the bill.

Permits in Denver

Denver and each suburb have their own right-of-way rules for a dumpster in the street. Private driveway is usually simpler. Check before you assume an alley is private. General rule, still true here: private driveway is often no street permit; public street usually is. Driveway vs street.

Do not load snow into the dumpster to “make room” on the drive — you pay to dump it. Roofing in hail season is a volume business here; book the box when you book the crew.
