Los Angeles, California

Dumpster rental in Los Angeles

Los Angeles County is a pile of cities, hills, and HOAs — not one dumpster rule.

The City of Los Angeles is not Santa Monica, Pasadena, Long Beach, Burbank, or unincorporated county land. Permit desks differ. Dry heat is the default; wind events and wildfire-season debris spikes are when boxes get scarce. Hillsides and canyon drives are an access problem for roll-off trucks.

HOAs and hillside CC&Rs regularly restrict where a box can sit and how long it can stay. Get that in writing. We do not publish a Los Angeles permit dollar amount.

Sizes that actually get used

Same US sizes as everywhere: 10, 15, 20, 30, and 40 cubic yards. Most Los Angeles home projects land on a 10-yard (bathroom, heavy tile/stucco) or a 20-yard (roof, cleanout, kitchen-plus). Tile roofs and stucco are heavy — ask about weight, not just yards. Size guide and chooser. Project notes: roof, kitchen, cleanout, concrete.

Pricing recap

A large, competitive market. Canyon mileage and heavy tile or stucco can push a quote up; the headline drop fee is not the whole bill. National context: many 10-yard weeks quote around $250–$500 and 20-yard weeks around $350–$700 before overage, extra days, and trip fees. Not a Los Angeles guarantee. What changes the bill.

Permits in Los Angeles

Use the city on your water bill. Unincorporated pockets go through Los Angeles County, not City Hall. Driveway on private property is usually simpler; on-street placement is a municipal permit question. General rule, still true here: private driveway is often no street permit; public street usually is. Driveway vs street.

Narrow canyon lots, low power lines, and gated drives are the usual dry-run causes. If the only drop is the street in front of an HOA, budget time for the association first, then the city.
