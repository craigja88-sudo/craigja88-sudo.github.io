Miami, Florida

Dumpster rental in Miami

Miami-Dade is hot, wet, and busy with remodels, condo work, and storm cleanup. A dumpster here is the same steel box as anywhere else — the local issues are heat, hurricane season, and who actually owns the pavement you want to block.

Summers are long, humid, and broken by afternoon storms. Wet household debris and storm-soaked junk weigh more than they look, which is how people blow a weight cap. Hurricane season runs June through November; after a named storm, dumpsters get scarce and quotes jump because landfills and haulers are slammed. If you can wait for a non-emergency cleanout, you usually should.

The City of Miami is not the same as Miami Beach, Coral Gables, Hialeah, or unincorporated Miami-Dade. Permit desks differ. We will not quote you a city fee. If the box sits on a private driveway, many places treat that as private property; if it sits in the street, assume you need the local right-of-way office and possibly police-detail rules on busier cuts. Condo associations and HOAs in South Florida are strict about where a box can sit and how long it can stay — get that in writing.

Sizes that actually get used

Same US sizes as everywhere: 10, 15, 20, 30, and 40 cubic yards. Most Miami home projects land on a 10-yard (bathroom, small deck, heavy debris) or a 20-yard (roof, cleanout, kitchen-plus). Size guide and chooser.

Pricing recap

South Florida often sits in the national bands on our pricing page, with spikes after storms and during heavy construction stretches. Ask every quote for included tons and days, not just the drop fee. National context: many 10-yard weeks quote around $250–$500 and 20-yard weeks around $350–$700 before overage, extra days, and trip fees. Not a Miami guarantee. What changes the bill.

Permits in Miami

Confirm with the City of Miami, Miami-Dade County, or your municipality — not with a national blog’s fee table. Driveway vs street is the first question; the association is the second. General rule, still true here: private driveway is often no street permit; public street usually is. Driveway vs street.

Low-clearance porte-cocheres, gated entries, and coral-rock / older driveways are common access problems. Tell the hauler about gates, overhead wires, and whether a truck can turn around. Salt air does not change the dumpster size you need; it does make “we’ll do it next month” a weaker plan on rusted patio and window projects you already scheduled.
