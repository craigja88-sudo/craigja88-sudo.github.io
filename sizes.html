Size guide

Dumpster sizes in plain terms

Homeowners say dumpster. The trucked steel box is a roll-off container, measured in cubic yards. One yard is a 3×3×3 cube. A 20-yard dumpster holds twenty of those.

10 CUBIC YARDS

Small jobs

Bathroom gut, small deck, closet and junk-room cleanout. Roughly 3 pickup-truck loads. Often the box haulers will allow for heavy material (concrete, dirt, brick) because weight adds up fast.

15 CUBIC YARDS

Mid-size remodel

Kitchen cabinets and drywall, a garage cleanout, or a bathroom plus another small room. A step up when a 10 looks tight in photos of similar jobs.

20 CUBIC YARDS

The default

Single-house asphalt roof, whole-house cleanout, flooring plus kitchen. If you only remember one size, remember 20. Still too small for a full gut of a large house.

30 CUBIC YARDS

Large remodel

Additions, multi-room guts, contractor debris over a week or two. Same footprint as many 20s, just taller — confirm your driveway clearance and HOA sight-line rules.

40 CUBIC YARDS

Construction

New builds and major tear-outs. Often swapped on a schedule, not a one-weekend rental. Overkill (and sometimes not allowed) for household junk or heavy masonry.

Weight beats volume

Shingles, plaster, tile, dirt, and concrete are dense. Haulers set a tonnage cap in the quote. Go over and you pay overage, or they will not pull the box until you unload. If the debris is heavy, book a smaller dumpster on purpose.

Do not mix prohibited items without asking: paint, solvents, batteries, tires, appliances with refrigerant, and anything the landfill will reject. Rules vary; the hauler’s list wins.
